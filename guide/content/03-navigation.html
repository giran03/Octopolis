Tabs, panes and paths

Move around quickly, keep many places open, and put two folders side by side.

The path bar

Every pane has a path bar made of clickable breadcrumbs. Each breadcrumb's arrow lists the folders next to it. Click an empty part of the bar, or press , to type a path instead: ~, %USERPROFILE% and even a file's path work, and suggestions appear as you type (subfolders of what you typed, then folders you use often).

Back, forward and up (Alt ←, Alt →, Alt ↑) return you to the folder you came from. Right-click Back or Forward to see the history lists.

Tabs

  • opens a tab, closes it, cycles, and reopens the last closed tab.
  • Middle-click (or Ctrl+click) a folder, breadcrumb or sidebar place to open it in a new tab; add Shift to open it in front.
  • Drag tabs to reorder them or move them to another pane. Right-click a tab to pin it, give it a color, duplicate it, close the others or move it to a new pane.

Panes arrange themselves in a split layout; the dividers can be dragged.

  • splits the window. F6 and Tab switch between panes; Shift F6 goes back.
  • Drag a file, folder or tab to the edge of a pane to open a new pane there. A skeleton previews it, the panes glide into place, and repeating it builds a grid of up to six panes.
  • Swap the panes or turn on synchronized browsing (buttons in the status bar) so going into a folder or up in one pane does the same in the other.
  • Compare the two panes: items only on one side, newer, older or a different size are marked, with Select differences and Copy or Move to the other pane.

The sidebar

  • Pinned folders you return to (drag a folder in, use + or right-click any folder), Quick access, Recent, Drives with free-space bars, Tags and Saved searches. Sections collapse, and the sidebar is resizable. Toggle it with .
  • Drives: an Eject button for USB drives, memory cards and discs; map and disconnect network drives; browse WSL distros.
  • The footer has buttons for tools such as Free up space and Custom actions.

What Octopolis remembers

Each folder remembers its view, icon size and sort (and grouping, if you use it). Folders you visit often and recently rise to the top of the palette. Folders update by themselves when files change outside the app.
